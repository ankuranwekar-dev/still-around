Your pet, still around.

Give it a few photos of your cat or dog. It measures their colours and markings and builds a small animated version of them that lives on your desktop — pottering about, dozing off, occasionally asking to be fed — while you get on with your day.

Click one of their eyes

This is the one thing worth doing by hand. Measuring an iris automatically goes wrong more often than it goes right — ginger fur is more saturated than most eyes, and in photographs pets are usually squinting. One click gets it exactly right.

The bright part is what it found. Clicking also re-cuts the photo from that point, which usually rescues a difficult one — or drag a box around your pet to be explicit.

    Why this exists

    This was built for two cats. Momo, a ginger-and-white tabby, died in 2025. Belle, a calico, died in May 2026. Their person wanted them still pottering about at the edge of the screen, so we made that — and then it seemed unkind to keep it to ourselves.

    So it is free, there is nothing to sign up to, and nothing about your animal is collected. If it helps, that is enough. If you want to put something in the tin, there is a link below, but the two of them would rather you fed a cat.

    What happens to your photos

    Nothing. There is no account, no upload, and no server that could receive them — this page is a handful of static files, and the measuring happens in your browser on your own machine. That includes the vision model that finds your pet: about 20 MB of weights are fetched once and cached, and then the model runs on your device, on your photographs, which never go anywhere. Close the tab and it is all gone. The pet file you can save is only the numbers: colours, how much white, how striped. Not your photographs.
